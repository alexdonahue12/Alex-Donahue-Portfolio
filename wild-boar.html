Project Brief

The original assignment was to design a full restaurant menu on a single page, with the name, cuisine, and typographic style all defined from scratch. I built Wild Boar around a family BBQ smokehouse in Milwaukee: a sit-down spot with sports-bar energy, made for game nights and big groups. The concept gave me an excuse to push the type and color hard.

Once the menu was finished, I expanded it into a complete restaurant identity: takeout packaging, storefront signage, a catering van, and staff uniforms. Every new piece had to follow the rules the menu set, and every one had to work within real production limits, not just look good on screen.

Brand System

The identity is loud on purpose. The wordmark is built from Monsterific BB, with the letters stretched and resized so they bounce inside a flame. For smaller spaces I drew a boar mark from scratch, with cream tusks and an angry squint, in versions for light and dark backgrounds and simple enough to embroider or cut from vinyl.

A burnt paper edge, spiky fire banners for headers and callouts, and soft smoke bubbles for short messages repeat across the pieces and tie them together.

Menu

A one-page menu sounds simple, but it’s really a hierarchy problem. Four categories, item names, prices, and promotional callouts all compete for the same space, so the type system has to do the organizing. The menu runs on five levels: logo, section headers, item names, prices, and callouts. Each level follows one consistent rule, so a diner can scan straight to what they want.

Takeout Set

The takeout set is where the system first left the page. The 9″ × 9″ × 3″ clamshell box carries the full-color logo on a cream base, with the burnt edge running along the lid. The 12″ × 7″ × 17″ kraft bag prints in a single red, using a knockout version of the logo so the paper shows through the letters, which keeps it cheap to produce. A 3″ badge sticker seals both, and its line, “Fresh off the smoker,” tells the customer the food was packed hot.

I built both pieces on real dielines, with bleed, trim, and crease lines marked, so the files are ready for a printer.

Storefront

The main sign is a 12′-0″ flame backer in brand red, with dark brown channel letters mounted 2″ off its face. Warm-white LEDs behind each letter create a halo, so the sign matches the logo in daylight and glows at night. I drew a dimensioned front elevation to show how the sign, door, and windows fit together on the building.

The door decal holds the badge, hours, services, and contact info, sized to a 30″ × 70″ glass panel with the hours at eye level. The badge is a printed decal, and everything else is cut from white vinyl. The two 6′ × 5′ window panes carry the flame logo with “Smoked in Milwaukee” and the badge with “BBQ · Brews · Game Day.”

Catering Van

The van wrap turns the flame into a wave that sweeps from behind the cab to the rear doors. The name and “BBQ catering for any crowd” sit on the red, and the phone number and website are placed so neither crosses a door gap and both read clearly against their background.

Staff Apron

The boar mark is embroidered on a dark brown canvas apron. On dark fabric, the mark’s dark features are just the fabric showing through, which keeps the stitching simple and the details clean.
